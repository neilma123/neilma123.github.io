I am an M.Eng. student in Financial Engineering at Cornell University. I plan to apply to full-time quantitative research roles and PhD programs in quantitative finance and applied mathematics.

My coursework and projects focus on stochastic calculus, rough volatility, heavy-tailed distributions, and Monte Carlo methods in Python and C++. Recently, I worked on simulating ruin and drawdown probabilities for heavy-tailed (α-stable) asset returns using Gaussian scale mixtures and importance sampling, testing sensitivity across tail indices α between 1.4 and 1.7. Outside of class, I am self-studying extreme value theory (EVT).

Previously, I interned as a quantitative analyst at BHG Financial, where I worked on customer segmentation pipelines and underwriter decision modeling. At Michigan, I was a research assistant modeling market exploration dynamics with neural networks, and I founded the Michigan Quantitative and Computational Finance Group.

SABR Calibration & Statistical Arbitrage

A production-shaped research prototype that translates SABR volatility modeling into C++, calibrates daily SPXW smiles from real bid/ask chains, and tests whether model residuals survive execution costs. ORIE 5610 • Cornell

Computation of Greeks under Rough Volterra Stochastic Volatility Models

Course paper and presentation implementing Malliavin calculus integration-by-parts to estimate option sensitivities (Delta and Gamma) under rough Volterra models (α-RFSV, rough Bergomi, rough Stein-Stein), benchmarked against finite difference and pathwise estimators.
