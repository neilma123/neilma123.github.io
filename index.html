I am a Master of Engineering candidate in Financial Engineering at Cornell University, actively pursuing both full-time quantitative research roles and PhD programs in quantitative finance, stochastic analysis, and applied mathematics.

My quantitative background spans stochastic calculus, heavy-tailed distributions, rough volatility, Bayesian inference, and high-performance computing in Python and C++. My recent academic projects and presentations focus on Heavy-Tailed Risk Modeling—modeling ruin and maximum drawdown probabilities under heavy-tailed (α-stable) return processes using Monte Carlo and importance sampling, deriving Gaussian scale-mixture representations to accelerate simulation, and analyzing sensitivity across tail index parameters α ∈ [1.4, 1.7]. In addition, I am actively self-studying Extreme Value Theory (EVT) and non-Gaussian limit distributions.

My previous industry and academic experience includes building production LLM-forward automation and underwriter decision probability models as a Quantitative Analyst Intern at BHG Financial, engineering feed-forward neural networks in Python to model exploration dynamics in multi-agent market simulations as a Research Assistant at the University of Michigan, and founding the 50+ member Quantitative and Computational Finance Group at U of M.

Computation of Greeks under Rough Volterra Stochastic Volatility Models

Formulates and implements Malliavin calculus integration-by-parts estimators for option sensitivities (Delta, Gamma) under non-Markovian rough Volterra models (α-RFSV, rough Bergomi, rough Stein-Stein) with comparison against finite difference and pathwise estimators.

Analysis of stochastic optimization algorithms, convergence rates, and Lipschitz continuous gradient complexity in finite and infinite-dimensional spaces, with applications to neural network training, maximum likelihood estimation, and optimal control.

Motivates fractional Brownian motion (fBM) in capturing hyperbolic volatility persistence and long-range dependence. Compares Cholesky decomposition, circulant embedding, Davies-Harte, and Wood-Chan simulation methods.
